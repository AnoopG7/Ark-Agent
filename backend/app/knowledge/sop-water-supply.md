# Emergency Drinking Water Supply

## Daily allowance
Plan **3 litres of drinking water per person per day** as the survival minimum. During hot or humid
weather, or for groups with many children, nursing mothers or elderly people, plan **4 litres per
person per day**. These figures cover drinking only; cooking and hygiene water (an additional
12 litres per person per day) is supplied separately through tanker points.

## Calculating a request
Multiply the number of people by the daily allowance and by the number of days to be covered.
A request "for 300 people by evening" covers one day unless stated otherwise:
300 people × 3 L = 900 L minimum. Round the total up to the nearest 20 L because water is issued
in 20-litre jerrycans.

## Splitting across depots
If no single depot can supply the full quantity over a usable route, split the allocation across
depots. Each part must travel on its own route that connects that depot to the ward. Record any
shortfall explicitly; never round a shortfall away.

## Quality and handling
Only sealed jerrycans or chlorinated tanker water may be distributed. Delivery teams must carry
chlorine tablets (one tablet per 20 L) in case jerrycans are refilled locally.

## Priority groups
Distribute first to households with infants under five, pregnant or nursing women, people with
disabilities and the elderly. Relief camps and hospitals take precedence over individual homes.
